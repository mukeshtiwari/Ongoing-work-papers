\title{Schulze in Zero Knowledge:\\ Publicly Tally-Hiding Ranked Elections from Certified $\Sigma$-Protocols}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Authors}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
In a ranked-choice election with $m$ candidates there are $m!$ possible
rankings, so a published ballot is often unique and identifies its voter
(the \emph{Italian attack}). In a small electorate, published pairwise
counts identify ballots too. Verifiable tallies for the Schulze method
publish one or the other. The tally-hiding systems that publish neither
rely on multi-party computation or on SNARKs with a trusted setup.

We present a verifiable Schulze election that publishes neither and uses
only $\Sigma$-protocols. The public learns the winner and the direction
of some head-to-head defeats, but no margin. A
ballot is a matrix of encrypted pairwise bits tied to an encrypted
ranking, and the voter proves it well formed with membership proofs. The pairwise counts are added up under encryption and never
decrypted on the bulletin board. The trustees prove the winner correct
with a zero-knowledge certificate of $O(m^3)$ proofs, whose size grows
linearly with the number of voters, so the scheme suits small
electorates, of the order of a hundred voters. Every proof comes from a certified
library of $\Sigma$-protocols in the Rocq theorem prover. The library has
machine-checked completeness, special soundness, and zero-knowledge for
the standard protocols and their $n$-ary compositions, and is extracted
to OCaml. We extract the client and the verifier
from these proofs and run them on the thirteen Gentoo council elections
of 2011--2023. They certify the first-seat winner of Gentoo's count in
every one.

The certified library is also a test oracle for deployed systems.
Comparing it with the Belenios verifier on the same election data
revealed a bug that accepted an invalid ballot. The Belenios maintainers
have since fixed it.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Ethics Considerations and Potential Harms}\label{sec:ethics}
The Belenios experiments ran on our machines against open-source
releases of \texttt{belenios-tool} and elections generated with it. They
involved no production system and no voter data. We reported the dropped
ballot check to the Belenios maintainers by e-mail, and they confirmed 
and fixed it the next day
(\texttt{115d0d47}).

\section*{Data Availability}
The Rocq development, the extracted code, the
public Gentoo ballots, and the scripts behind every number are at
\url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/SigmaProtocol-EBA5}. We will
release them under an open-source licence.
