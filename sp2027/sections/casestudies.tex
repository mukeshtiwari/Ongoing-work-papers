\subsection{Belenios}\label{sec:cs:belenios}
We compare \texttt{belenios-tool election verify} (release 3.2.0), the
verifier the Belenios specification prescribes to auditors, with our
certified verifier on five elections generated with \texttt{belenios-tool}
itself and on 961 honest, mutated, structural and forged boards; the
forgeries are consistent archives with re-signed events, built by an
independent implementation of the specification.

\paragraph{A dropped check}
On the honest elections both verifiers agree and accept, and all 749
field mutants are rejected by both. The disagreements concentrate in the
forge stage: \texttt{election verify} accepts 36 boards that our verifier
rejects. Each contains a single invalid ballot, a well-formed ciphertext
encrypting a value outside the allowed range with random proofs and a
random signature under a listed credential, inside an otherwise fully
consistent archive (hash chain, encrypted tally, trustee decryption
proofs and result all recomputed to match). The ballot is counted, and
the audit command reports \emph{``all checks passed''}; Belenios's own
per-ballot checker, \texttt{election verify-ballot}, and our verifier
reject it. The cause is one line of \texttt{src/tool/tool\_election.ml}:
the \texttt{verify} command forces the lazy ballot-verification
computation but discards the resulting promise, \texttt{ignore (Lazy.force
verified\_ballots)}, so the rejection raised for an invalid ballot is
never observed (Lwt does not report rejected promises that nobody
awaits), and the per-ballot signature and proof checks have no effect on
the verdict; the \texttt{--skip-ballot-check} flag discards the same
promise and was already a no-op. Under the universal-verifiability threat
model, where the server builds the published archive, this lets a server
count ballots with invalid proofs or signatures, stuffing a ballot under
a credential that never voted or overwriting a voter's ballot, while the
official audit tool reports success; no trustee secret is required. The
defect was introduced in 2023 and is present in releases 3.1 through
3.3.0. We reported it to the maintainers, who confirmed it in 3.3.0,
attributed it to the same cause (``an exception not being propagated
properly''), and committed a fix (\texttt{115d0d4}) that forces the
ballot computation under \texttt{Lwt.try\_bind} and re-raises its
exception; the patched tool rejects the previously accepted boards with
\texttt{InvalidBallot} and still accepts honest elections. The web
server, which checks ballots on submission, was not affected; the defect
was confined to the offline command an auditor is directed to run.

\paragraph{The mix-net and a missing subgroup check}
Belenios's non-homomorphic questions are mixed with a
Terelius--Wikstr\"om proof of a shuffle~\cite{haenni2017pseudocode} over
Ed25519, a curve of cofactor~8. The extracted certified
Terelius--Wikstr\"om verifier, run at Ed25519 with no new proof (it is
parametric in the group and takes its challenges as closures), accepts
\texttt{belenios-tool}'s own shuffles. But \texttt{check\_shuffle\_proof}
applies the subgroup test \texttt{G.check} to the proof's commitments and
never to its input or output ciphertexts, although Belenios applies it to
ciphertexts everywhere else. A mix server that adds a point of order~2 or
8 to the $\beta$ component of one output and proves the shuffle honestly
is accepted by \texttt{election verify} for a fraction of seeds that
depends on a parity of the Fiat--Shamir challenge (26 of 100 for order~2,
2 of 100 for order~8); the torsion survives the honest re-encryptions of a
chain of servers and is caught only at decryption. Over 754 mix-net
cases, \texttt{belenios-tool} accepts 97 that the certified pipeline
rejects, and nothing in the other direction: a soundness gap in the audit
tool's shuffle verdict (medium), not a result forgery. \TODO{Disclosure
of this finding; Section~\ref{sec:ethics} currently covers only the
dropped ballot check.} Fed the same tampered shuffles with its decoder's
subgroup check bypassed on purpose, the extracted certified verifier
accepts them at the same rate as Belenios: the verified algebra does not
reject a torsion point, because in Rocq the group type \emph{is} the
order-$\ell$ subgroup and extraction erases that proof. The certified
pipeline rejects all of them at its decoder, which checks the order of
every point before anything reaches the certified code. This is the
sharpest instance of Section~\ref{sec:lessons}: the soundness of an
extracted verifier rests on its decoder enforcing the group the proofs
assume.

\subsection{Swiss Post}\label{sec:cs:swisspost}
The Swiss Post system is used for Swiss voters abroad; its 2019 version
was withdrawn after flaws in its proof of shuffle~\cite{9152765}, and the
current version publishes its source, specifications and a bug bounty.
We pinned \texttt{crypto-primitives}~1.6.1.0 (Java, specified in
pseudocode~\cite{swisspost_cryptoprimitives_spec}), the
Verifier~1.7.1.0~\cite{swisspost_verifier_spec} and the
\texttt{testdata}~1.6.1.0 repository~\cite{swisspost_testdata}, and
instantiated a certified verifier and prover for the Bayer--Groth shuffle
argument~\cite{10.1007/978-3-642-29011-4_17} (machine-checked
before~\cite{287095}) and the library's verifier for its four other
proofs at the 3072-bit group of the test vectors, with Coqprime
certificates for $p$ and $q$. On the shuffle argument (nine shapes,
including a key longer than the ciphertexts) both verifiers accept every
honest argument and the recomputed challenges equal Swiss Post's, every
one of 1{,}224 field mutants is rejected by both, arguments of our
extracted prover with degenerate randomness and identity permutation are
accepted by Swiss Post's verifier, and both reject every proof of a false
shuffle; the production mixing path agrees with the certified verifier on
154 cases up to $1000$ ciphertexts. For each of the seven bound checks of
Table~\ref{tab:bgchecks}, on five shapes, the control is accepted and the
forgery rejected by both verifiers (66 cases), and the Swiss Post verifier
reports exactly the targeted check; every bound check is present and
necessary. The four other proofs agree on 7{,}223 cases. For the synthetic
election \texttt{Post\_E2E\_DEV} our protocol wiring verifies all 20
shuffles, 60 decryption proofs and 57 checks without proofs in 12\,s,
and of 29 tampered copies re-signed with the published development keys
(boxes swapped, votes dropped, duplicated, added or reordered, proofs or
decryptions changed, a final mix redone under a fresh key) the Verifier
rejects all but the reordering the specification sorts, and the certified
side agrees on the 21 scenarios in its scope. We found no soundness
defect and five low-severity ones: lenient Base64/Base32 decoders where
the specification assumes injective encodings, six constructors that
throw the wrong exception on empty vectors, decoding evaluated after a
failed proof so that the auditor sees an unexpected error, payloads
refused by the domain classes reported as errors rather than failed
checks, and a tally manifest naming another election that the Verifier
does not read.

\subsection{Helios}\label{sec:cs:helios}
We compare Helios's own Python cryptography (helios-server, commit
\texttt{88621e3}), driven through its verification path, with our
certified verifier on five generated elections whose ballots come half
from the booth's JavaScript and half from our certified prover, and on
the IACR 2023 and 2024 elections. Each verifier accepts the other side's
honest ballots. Over 734 cases nothing is accepted by the certified
verifier and rejected by Helios; Helios accepts 70 inputs that the
certified verifier rejects, 55 by deliberate strictness on our side
(canonical JSON only) and 15 from four low-severity leniencies of Helios:
extra individual proofs and extra decryption factors are ignored, proof
responses are not reduced or range-checked, and integers are parsed
leniently, so that many encodings of one ballot verify. None changes a
vote or a tally.

\subsection{Verificatum}\label{sec:cs:verificatum}
Verificatum~\cite{verificatum} is Wikstr\"om's mix-net; its proof of a
shuffle is the Terelius--Wikstr\"om
argument~\cite{terelius2010restricted,10.1007/978-3-642-02620-1_28} and
auditors run its stand-alone verifier \texttt{vmnv} (VMN and VCR 3.1.0,
pure Java). The certified verifier implements the specification's
Algorithms~19, 22, 25 and~27 at the certified 2048-bit group; the trusted
boundary parses byte trees, derives generators and computes the two
SHA-256 oracles. On sessions produced by \texttt{vmn}'s own demo scripts
with seeded randomness, 79 cases per session (honest, typed leaf
mutations, structural and encoding mutants, protocol tampering, a crafted
collision of first announcements), the verifiers agree on 75; the four
disagreements have \texttt{vmnv} accepting and the certified verifier
rejecting, and each is sound: a corrupted decryption reply of a server
outside the threshold subset \texttt{vmnv} combines, and a trailing
element in a proof array (one low). No forged proof is accepted by either
verifier. The campaign also found a completeness bug in our own boundary,
a table keyed by a non-unique announcement (Section~\ref{sec:lessons}).
