\section{Comparison to Cortier et al.}\label{app:cortier}
Table~\ref{tab:cortier} sets our trustees against the tally-hiding
Condorcet--Schulze protocol of Cortier et al.~\cite{cgy2022}, using the
only benchmarks published for it~\cite[Table 14]{yang2023thesis}. They
report the trustees' wall-clock time and the transcript size, for three
trustees of four computing threads each, and no verification time. Our
numbers are those of Table~\ref{tab:par}: the trustees' wall-clock time
with twelve worker processes, and the certificate size. At their sixth
size, twenty candidates and $1024$ voters ($8$ hours $50$ minutes,
$16$\,GB), we did not run our prover.

The comparison is indicative only. The two protocols do not hide the
same thing: their talliers learn only the winner, while ours learn $D$
and the public learns the witness paths. The machines differ (two EPYC
7282 processors there, an Apple M5 Max here), their implementation works
over an elliptic curve and ours over a 2048-bit group, and their
transcript covers the whole tally while our certificate covers only the
proof of the winner. Having said this, at the five sizes both papers
report, our trustees take between a tenth and three tenths of their
time, and our certificate is between a third and a half of their
transcript.
