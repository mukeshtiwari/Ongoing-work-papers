\section{Shared Key and Extensions of the Certificate}\label{app:extensions}
\textbf{Shared key.} The mechanised tally decrypts with a single trustee
key. A deployment shares the key. Trustee $i$ holds $x_i$ and publishes
$h_i = g^{x_i}$ with a Schnorr proof of knowledge, which prevents a
rogue-key attack. The election key is $h = h_1 \cdots h_n$. Ballots,
their proofs, and aggregation use only $h$, so their theorems hold
unchanged. Only part (i) of the certificate uses the secret key. With a
shared key, part (i) becomes one partial decryption per trustee, each
with a Chaum--Pedersen proof against $h_i$. The library's
\rocq{DistElgamal} module proves that the partial decryptions combine
correctly. Parts (ii)--(iv) need only the randomness of the fresh
encryptions and do not change. One lemma is not yet mechanised: that $n$
accepted partial-decryption proofs imply a correct combined decryption.
The sharing is $n$-of-$n$: internal privacy needs only one honest
trustee, but every trustee must take part in the tally. A $t$-of-$n$ sharing would
add Shamir shares and Lagrange interpolation.

Section~\ref{sec:discussion} names three limits of the certificate. The
rest of this appendix sketches how each can be lifted. None of it is
implemented or mechanised.

\textbf{Size logarithmic in $N$.} Two changes would replace the factor
$N$ by $\log N$. The first uses only proofs that the library already
has. To show $u \ge u'$ for encrypted $u, u' \in [0, N]$, the trustees
encrypt the bits of $u - u'$, prove each ciphertext an encryption of $0$
or $1$, and choose the randomness so that the verifier can recombine the
bits under encryption into $\mathrm{Enc}(u)/\mathrm{Enc}(u')$. A range
proof then has $2\lceil \log_2 (N+1) \rceil$ branches instead of $N +
1$. A closure disjunction ``$p_{ij} \ge p_{ik}$ or $p_{ij} \ge p_{kj}$''
becomes an encrypted value $\mu$ with a two-branch proof that $\mu$
equals $p_{ik}$ or $p_{kj}$, and one such range proof of $p_{ij} \ge
\mu$. The second change is the batching of Section~\ref{sec:batched}.
The trustees know the openings of $d$ and $p$. So every range check of
Section~\ref{sec:tally} can be written as a bit decomposition of a linear
combination of committed vectors, which one zero argument proves, and
each closure disjunction becomes a committed minimum with one product
check. The certificate would then be $O(m^3 \log N)$ field elements
instead of $O(m^3 N)$ group elements.

\textbf{No witness paths.} The certificate publishes paths because the
lower bounds of part (iii) have many solutions, and the trustees must
show that $p$ is the least one. They could instead certify the $m$ steps
of Floyd--Warshall (Section~\ref{sec:bg:schulze}): publish every
intermediate matrix $p^{(k)}$ in encrypted form, with $p^{(0)} = d$, and
prove for every entry that $p^{(k)}_{ij} = \max(p^{(k-1)}_{ij}, \mu)$
with $\mu = \min(p^{(k-1)}_{ik}, p^{(k-1)}_{kj})$. A maximum is a
two-branch equality proof and two range proofs, and so is a minimum.
This costs about $2m^3$ more ciphertexts and their proofs and shows no
value in clear, so the public would learn the winner only.

\textbf{Ties.} To certify that $w$ is the first Schulze winner in
candidate order, the trustees would add, for every earlier candidate
$l$, a proof that $l$ is not a winner: some $j$ has $p_{jl} > p_{lj}$.
This is a disjunction over $j$ of membership proofs of $p_{jl} - p_{lj}
\in \{1, \ldots, N\}$, which hides $j$.

\textbf{Several seats.} Gentoo's council fills several seats from one
ranked election: it removes the winner and runs the method again on the
remaining candidates. The certificate supports this without touching the
ballots. Restricting $\mathrm{Enc}(D)$ to the remaining candidates gives
the aggregate of the smaller election, and the trustees certify a winner
of it. Seven seats need seven certificates on the same encrypted counts,
with $m, m-1, \ldots, m-6$ candidates, and each adds its own witness
paths to the leakage. We have implemented and measured the first seat
only.
