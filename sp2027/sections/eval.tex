\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:eval}
A script of the artefact, named in each table caption, produces every
number in this section. All measurements are single-threaded on an
Apple M5 Max laptop (48\,GB RAM) with the extracted OCaml code compiled
by OCaml 5.4.1, arbitrary-precision arithmetic through
\texttt{zarith} (GMP), and SHAKE-256 from HACL$^*$ as the Fiat--Shamir
hash.

\subsection{The leak, measured}\label{sec:eval:leak}
Gentoo elects its Council (seven seats, yearly) and its Foundation
Trustees with a Condorcet count. Voters rank the candidates with ties,
unlisted candidates rank last, and \texttt{countify} applies Schwartz
sequential dropping (the Schulze procedure of the Debian Constitution,
A.5 steps 5--8, with no quorum, majority, or default option) repeatedly
to produce a ranking, from which the seats are filled. Gentoo publishes every ballot under an anonymous confirmation id, so
the archive shows what a published ballot or count matrix gives a
coercer in the kind of election our scheme targets. On all $41$
published elections (2005--2023), the Schulze winner computed by the
script of Table~\ref{tab:leak} coincides with Gentoo's own first-pass
winner. Table~\ref{tab:leak} reports, for the thirteen council elections
of 2011--2023, the number of ballots $N$ and candidates $m$ (including
the \texttt{\_reopen\_nominations} option), the number of ballots whose
ranking, as a position vector, is cast by no other voter, and the number
of candidate pairs whose margin $|D_{ij} - D_{ji}|$ is at most two. In
every election, all but at most two ballots are unique. A coercer who
prescribed a ranking therefore identifies the voter on a published board
with certainty, and a published count matrix confirms a prescribed
preference on up to ten pairs per election. Our certificate publishes
neither. Its witness paths reveal the direction, but not the margin, of
$118$ of the $1050$ pairs, and they reveal nothing in 2016 and 2023
(Section~\ref{sec:security}).

\subsection{Ballot cost}\label{sec:eval:ballot}
A ballot for $m$ candidates has $m + m^2$ ciphertexts and $m + 2m^2$
proofs (Section~\ref{sec:ballot}): $2m(m+2)(2m+1)$ group elements and
$m(2m+3) + m^2(4m+8)$ field elements in all, i.e.\ $512\,(m+2)(2m+1)m +
32\,(4m^3 + 10m^2 + 3m)$ bytes at the 2048-bit Helios parameters.
Table~\ref{tab:ballotcost} lists the sizes for $m$ in the range of the
Gentoo elections, with the proving and verification times of the
extracted OCaml.

\subsection{Tally cost}\label{sec:eval:tally}
The certificate for $m$ candidates and $N$ accepted ballots has $m^2$
re-encryptions with decryption proofs, $m^2$ encrypted defeats with
$(N{+}1)$-branch range proofs and $(2N{+}1)$-branch defeat disjunctions,
$m^2$ encrypted path strengths with range proofs, $m^3$ closure
disjunctions of $2N{+}2$ branches, at most $m^2(m-1)$ edge proofs along
the witness paths, and $m-1$ winner proofs (Section~\ref{sec:tally}).
Table~\ref{tab:tallycost} gives its size and the trustees' and the
verifier's time, as a function of $m$ at fixed $N$ and of $N$ at fixed
$m$. A council election fills seven seats from the ranking. With our scheme
the trustees certify the winner, remove it, and certify again on the
same encrypted counts, so seven certificates on $m, m-1, \ldots$
candidates replace Gentoo's seven passes. The certificate grows linearly
in $N$ through the branch counts and cubically in $m$ through the closure
disjunctions. The measurements of Table~\ref{tab:tallycost} fit this, at
$7$--$14$\,ms per disjunction branch for the trustees (whose time
includes decrypting the counts by a search up to $N$) and
$3$--$4$\,ms for the verifier.

\subsection{The Gentoo council elections, end to end}\label{sec:eval:gentoo}
We ran the thirteen council elections of Table~\ref{tab:leak} through
the extracted code, starting from the published rankings. The client of
Section~\ref{sec:ballot} (the first ballot) encrypts each voter's
position vector under a credential-bound oracle and posts it to a board
file. The verifier checks every ballot, replays the first ballot under
another credential and checks that it is rejected, and aggregates. The
trustees' prover then decrypts the counts and certifies the winner, and
the verifier checks the certificate (\rocqfile[Executable/SchulzeGentoocode]{src/Executable/SchulzeGentoocode}, two programs so
that an interrupted run loses nothing). We then ran the same elections
through the batched ballot of Section~\ref{sec:batched}
(\rocqfile[gentoo3.exe]{src/Executable/SchulzeGentoocode/gentoo3.ml}, voters and verifier in one process, since a
ballot now takes seconds), with the same aggregation and certificate.
Table~\ref{tab:gentoo} reports the medians per ballot for both ballots
and the certificate times. In every election every ballot is accepted, the replayed ballot is
rejected, the certificate verifies, and the certified winner is the
Schulze winner of the published rankings, which is Gentoo's. The batched
runs accept the same ballots and certify the same winners. The thirteen
elections ran concurrently, one process each, on an $18$-core laptop that
was also in interactive use, so the certificate times of
Table~\ref{tab:gentoo} are wall-clock upper bounds. We re-ran the $2013$,
$2015$, and $2023$ elections with processor-time measurement (the $2023$ one alone, the other two as a
pair), and their rows report those runs (for $2023$ the trustees' $18$
minutes and the verifier's $9$ minutes are within $3$\% of the
wall-clock of a run on an idle machine). Ballot verification takes
$12$\,s per ballot at $10$ candidates and $57$\,s at $17$. The
certificate, whose branch counts grow with $N$ and whose proof count
grows with $m^3$, takes the trustees $18$ minutes at $(m, N) = (10, 50)$,
$25$ minutes at $(10, 72)$, and $144$ minutes at $(17, 96)$, and its
verifier about half of that. With the batched ballot the voters' side of
an election takes minutes: the three-pass client proves a
ballot in $0.8$\,s at ten candidates and $2.9$\,s at seventeen, the
verifier checks it in $0.5$ to $1.6$\,s, and the board files
(decimal text) shrink from $175$\,MB--$1.5$\,GB to $8$--$43$\,MB. The certificate, which does not
depend on the ballot design, then makes up the whole cost of the election.

\subsection{The library as a test oracle}\label{sec:eval:oracle}
The certified prover takes its randomness and witness as inputs, so it
can produce proofs of false statements with correctly derived Fiat--Shamir
challenges. On 1{,}715 honest, mutated, structural, and forged boards,
Belenios's audit tool (\texttt{belenios-tool election verify}, release
3.2.0) accepted 36 forged boards, each with one invalid ballot, and
reported ``all checks passed'', because one line discarded the per-ballot check
results. The maintainers fixed it the day after our report. Their web server
was unaffected. A missing subgroup test in the mix-net shuffle
verifier is still to be reported \TODO{report this finding}. Appendix~\ref{sec:belenios}
gives the details.
