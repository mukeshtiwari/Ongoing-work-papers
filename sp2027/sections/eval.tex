\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:eval}
Scripts in the artefact produce every number in this section. Every
program but the parallel prover of Section~\ref{sec:eval:tally} uses
one core, and we ran them on an Apple M5 Max laptop with 48\,GB of RAM. Sizes count $256$ bytes per group element and $32$
per field element. The extracted OCaml code is compiled with OCaml 5.4.1. It
uses \texttt{zarith} (GMP) for big-number arithmetic and SHAKE-256 from
HACL$^*$ as the Fiat--Shamir hash. Appendix~\ref{app:backends} runs a
smaller approval ballot through Peregrine~\cite{peregrine} (C and CakeML) and CertiRocq
(WebAssembly).


\subsection{Ballot cost}\label{sec:eval:ballot}
Table~\ref{tab:ballotcost} lists the size of both ballots for $m$ in
the range of the Gentoo elections, with the proving and verification
times of the extracted OCaml code (medians over repeated runs, one core).

\textbf{First ballot.} The first ballot for $m$ candidates has $m + m^2$
ciphertexts and $m + 2m^2$ proofs (Section~\ref{sec:ballot}). A proof
with $k$ branches has $k$ announcements, each a pair of group elements,
and $2k+1$ challenges and responses. Each of the $m$ position ciphertexts
carries a proof with $m+1$ branches, and each of the $m^2$ bit
ciphertexts a $0/1$ proof and a consistency proof with $2m+1$ branches.
In all, that is $2m(m+2)(2m+1)$ group elements and $m(2m+3) +
m^2(4m+8)$ field elements, or $512\,(m+2)(2m+1)m + 32\,(4m^3 + 10m^2 +
3m)$ bytes at the 2048-bit Helios parameters. The consistency proofs
dominate: they hold about $2m^3$ of the branches, so size and time grow
with $m^3$. 

\textbf{Batched ballot.} The batched ballot has the $m^2$ ciphertexts,
$L + 3$ commitments, the linear proof, and the zero argument
(Section~\ref{sec:batched}). In all, that is $2m^2 + 3L + 17$ group
elements and $3m^2 + m + 7$ field elements, with $L = \lceil \log_2
(m+1) \rceil$. Beyond the ciphertexts themselves, the proofs add about
three field elements per cell and a few dozen group elements. At $m =
17$ the ciphertexts take $145$\,KB of the ballot's $180$\,KB. Against
the first ballot, the batched ballot is $35$ times smaller at $m = 17$,
proves $47$ times faster, and verifies about $9$ times faster. Unlike
the first ballot, it takes longer to verify than to prove. Even so, the
board checks a seventeen-candidate ballot in under $2$\,s.

\subsection{Tally cost}\label{sec:eval:tally}
Table~\ref{tab:tallycost} (Appendix~\ref{app:onecore}) gives the
certificate's size and time on synthetic elections with the extracted
prover. The certificate grows linearly in $N$, through the
branch counts of its membership proofs, and cubically in $m$, through the
$m^3$ closure disjunctions (Section~\ref{sec:tally}). 

The extracted prover is slow for a reason unrelated to the
cryptography: it runs on one core and computes every proof twice, once
to get the announcements that Fiat--Shamir hashes, and again with the
resulting challenge. A second prover,
\rocqfile[par.exe]{src/Executable/SchulzeBenchcode/par.ml}, calls the
same extracted functions, but computes each proof once, on twelve
worker processes. An announcement does not depend on the challenge, so
it can fill in the challenge and the cheap responses afterwards. Only
this outer loop is unverified. On small elections it produces exactly
the extracted prover's certificate for the same randomness.
Table~\ref{tab:par} gives its times: about $1.5$\,ms of processor time per
branch. The extracted verifier accepted all five of these certificates.

\subsection{The Gentoo council elections, end to end}\label{sec:eval:gentoo}
We ran the thirteen Gentoo council elections through the extracted code,
starting from the published rankings
(\rocqfile[Executable/SchulzeGentoocode]{src/Executable/SchulzeGentoocode}).
Each voter's client encrypts the ballot, with a Fiat--Shamir hash bound
to the voter's credential, and posts it to the board. The verifier checks every ballot and
aggregates, the trustees decrypt the counts and certify the winner, and
the verifier checks the certificate. As a replay test, the verifier also
resubmits the first ballot under another credential. We ran every
election once with the first ballot and once with the batched ballot
(\rocqfile[gentoo3.exe]{src/Executable/SchulzeGentoocode/gentoo3.ml}),
with the same aggregation and certificate.

In every election every ballot is accepted, the replayed ballot is
rejected, the certificate verifies, and the certified winner is the
Schulze winner of the published rankings, which is Gentoo's.
Table~\ref{tab:gentoo} gives the times. With the first ballot, proving
takes $5.6$--$27$\,s and verifying $3.0$--$14$\,s per ballot. The batched
ballot proves in $0.6$--$2.3$\,s and verifies in $0.4$--$1.6$\,s. The
certificate is the same for both ballots, and with the batched ballot it
is most of the cost: the trustees take $4.8$ minutes at $(m, N) = (10, 50)$ and $41$ minutes
at $(17, 96)$, and the verifier about half of that. 
We certify the first seat only (Section~\ref{sec:tally}).

\subsection{The library as a test oracle}\label{sec:eval:oracle}
The certified prover takes its randomness and its witness as inputs, so
it can also produce proofs of false statements with correctly derived
Fiat--Shamir challenges. On 1{,}715 honest, mutated, structural, and
forged boards, Belenios's audit tool (\texttt{belenios-tool election
verify}, release 3.2.0) accepted 36 forged boards, each with one invalid
ballot, and reported ``all checks passed''. The cause was one line that
discarded the results of the per-ballot checks. The maintainers fixed it
the day after our report. Their web server was not affected.
